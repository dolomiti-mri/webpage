First module · aEyes-knee

Knee MRI is high-volume,
and the read is the bottleneck

Volume

Three planes, dozens of slices, every study

Knee MRI is among the most frequent examinations. Scanner numbers keep growing; radiologist capacity does not grow with them.

Decisions

Two findings route a patient to surgery

ACL and meniscal tears. In the Stanford MRNet reader study, model assistance raised expert ACL specificity by 4.8 points — about 5 in every 100 healthy patients spared an unnecessary surgical work-up.

Access

New scanners, same reporting capacity

Praha-východ has more than 200,000 inhabitants and no local MRI. Our centre needs fast, consistent reads from day one.

Source: Bien et al., PLOS Medicine 2018 (MRNet reader study)

How it works

DICOM in,
structured second opinion out

01

DICOM study

Sagittal, coronal and axial series straight from the scanner or PACS.

02

Slice encoder

A pretrained 2D backbone with 2.5D context produces one embedding per slice.

03

Series pooling

Attention-MIL or transformer pooling — the model learns which slices matter.

04

Plane fusion

All planes fused into probabilities: abnormality, ACL tear, meniscal tear.

05

Second opinion

Probabilities, slice-level attention maps and a structured report back to PACS/RIS.
