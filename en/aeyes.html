First module · aEyes-knee

Knee MRI: more scans every year,
and reporting can't keep up

Volume

Three planes, dozens of slices in every study

Knee MRI is among the most frequent MRI examinations. Scanner numbers keep growing; radiologist numbers do not grow with them.

Decisions

Two findings decide what happens next

An ACL tear and a meniscal tear decide between conservative treatment and surgery. From the literature: in the Stanford MRNet reader study, model assistance raised clinicians' specificity for ACL tears by 4.8 percentage points: fewer false positives in patients without a tear.

Source: Bien et al., PLOS Medicine 2018 (reader study of the MRNet model, not of aEyes)

Validation status

A prototype at TRL 4

TRL 4 means technology validated in a laboratory environment. For aEyes-knee that is validation on public data, not in clinical operation.

Data

Four public knee MRI datasets

MRNet (Stanford): radiologist labels for the whole study. RSNA Knee Abnormality Detection: findings extracted from report text. fastMRI+ (NYU): bounding boxes around meniscal damage. KneeMRI (Clinical Hospital Centre Rijeka): grades of anterior cruciate ligament injury. Each dataset carries a different type of annotation.

Architecture

An ensemble of models over three planes

A convolutional neural network reads individual slices, separately in the sagittal, coronal and axial plane. Outputs of several independently trained models are averaged, and the three planes are combined into one result per finding.

Results

AUROC 0.91 · 0.94 · 0.87

Cross-validation on the MRNet training set (1,130 studies): abnormal finding 0.91, ACL tear 0.94, meniscal tear 0.87. Transfer without fine-tuning to data from another hospital (KneeMRI Rijeka, 230 studies): ACL tear 0.82. AUROC is the area under the ROC curve.

Still to do

Independent test sets and children's knees

We have not yet used the official MRNet validation set or the locked parts of the other datasets, and the model is not calibrated. The datasets do not focus on children and adolescents: whether the model carries over to their knees is a hypothesis that we will test on data from partner hospitals.

How it works

From images
to a second read

01

Knee MRI study

A standard examination in three planes (sagittal, coronal, axial), as DICOM straight from PACS.

02

Every slice

A neural network processes each slice together with its neighbouring slices.

03

The whole series

The model learns which slices matter and weights them accordingly. Those are the slices it later shows the radiologist.

04

Three planes together

Results from all planes are combined into three probabilities: abnormal finding, ACL tear, meniscal tear.

05

Second read

Probabilities, highlighted slices and a structured report go back into the tools the radiologist already reports in.
